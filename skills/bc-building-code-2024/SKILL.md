This skill provides detailed reference for **BCBC 2024, Division B, Part 9** (Housing and Small
Buildings). All section numbers, table values, and dimensional requirements below are authoritative
for answering compliance questions. Always cite the specific article (e.g., "per Article 9.8.4.1")
when giving code-based answers.

> of Areas and Spaces), 9.6 (Glass), 9.7 (Windows, Doors and Skylights), 9.8 (Stairs, Ramps,

## Section 9.3 — Materials

### 9.3.1 Concrete

#### 9.3.1.1 — Flat Insulating Concrete Form (ICF) Walls

For flat ICF walls not exceeding **2 storeys** in building height and having a maximum floor-to-floor height of **3 m**, in buildings of light-frame construction, the concrete and reinforcing shall comply with Part 4, or:
- Concrete shall conform to **CSA A23.1** "Concrete materials and methods of concrete construction," with a **maximum aggregate size of 19 mm**, and
- Reinforcing shall:
  - Conform to **CSA G30.18** "Carbon steel bars for concrete reinforcement"
  - Have a minimum specified yield strength of **400 MPa**, and
  - Be lapped a minimum of **450 mm** for 10M bars and **650 mm** for 15M bars (see also Articles 9.15.4.5 and 9.20.17.2 to 9.20.17.4)

#### 9.3.1.2 — Cement

Cement shall meet the requirements of **CSA A3001** "Cementitious Materials for Use in Concrete."

#### 9.3.1.3 — Concrete in Contact with Sulphate Soil

Concrete in contact with sulphate soil (which is deleterious to normal cement) shall conform to the requirements of **Clause 4.1.1.6 of CSA A23.1**.

#### 9.3.1.4 — Aggregates

Aggregates shall:
- Consist of sand, gravel, crushed rock, crushed air-cooled blast furnace slag, expanded shale or expanded clay conforming to **CSA A23.1**, and
- Be clean, well-graded and free of injurious amounts of organic and other deleterious material.

#### 9.3.1.5 — Water

Water shall be clean and free of injurious amounts of oil, organic matter, sediment or any other deleterious material.

#### 9.3.1.6 — Compressive Strength

Compressive strength of unreinforced concrete after **28 days** shall be not less than:
- **15 MPa** — walls, columns, fireplaces and chimneys, footings, foundation walls, grade beams and piers
- **20 MPa** — floors other than those in garages and carports
- **32 MPa** — garage and carport floors, and exterior steps (or **30 MPa** where indigenous aggregates do not achieve 32 MPa with a 0.45 water-to-cementing material ratio)

Site-batched concrete used for garage/carport floors and exterior steps shall have **air entrainment of 5 to 8%**.

#### 9.3.1.7 — Concrete Mixes

Maximum water-to-cementing materials ratio by weight (for pre-mixed and site-batched concrete):
- **0.70** — walls, columns, fireplaces and chimneys, footings, foundation walls, grade beams and piers
- **0.65** — floors other than those in garages and carports
- **0.45** — garage and carport floors, and exterior steps

**Table 9.3.1.7 — Site-Batched Concrete Mixes** (1 part cementing material = 1 × 40 kg bag):

| Max Coarse Aggregate Size (mm) | Cementing Material (Parts) | Fine Aggregate (damp avg coarse sand) Parts / L | Coarse Aggregate (gravel or crushed stone) Parts / L |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 1 | 1.75 / 28 | 2 / 56 |
| 20 | 1 | 1.75 / 28 | 2.5 / 70 |
| 28 | 1 | 2 / 56 | 3 / 84 |
| 40 | 1 | 2 / 56 | 3.5 / 98 |

Size of aggregate in unreinforced site-batched mixes shall not exceed:
- **1/5** the distance between the sides of vertical forms, or
- **1/3** the thickness of flatwork

#### 9.3.1.8 — Admixtures

Admixtures shall conform to:
- **ASTM C260** "Standard Specification for Air-Entraining Admixtures for Concrete," or
- **ASTM C494/C494M** "Standard Specification for Chemical Admixtures for Concrete," as applicable.

#### 9.3.1.9 — Cold Weather Requirements

When air temperature is below **5°C**, concrete shall be:
- Kept at a temperature of not less than **10°C** or more than **25°C** while being mixed and placed, and
- Maintained at a temperature of not less than **10°C** for **72 h** after placing.

No frozen material or ice shall be used in concrete.

#### 9.3.2.2 — Lumber Grades

Except for joists, rafters, trusses and beams, visually graded lumber shall conform to the grades in Table 9.3.2.1. (See Article 9.23.4.2 for joists, rafters and beams, and Article 9.23.14.11 for trusses.)

#### 9.3.2.3 — Machine Stress Rated Lumber

Machine stress rated lumber shall conform to the requirements of **Subsection 4.3.1**.

#### 9.3.2.4 — OSB, Waferboard and Plywood Marking

OSB, waferboard and plywood used for roof sheathing, wall sheathing and subflooring shall be legibly identified on the face of the material indicating:
- The manufacturer of the material
- The standard to which it is produced, and
- That the material is of an exterior type.

#### 9.3.2.5 — Moisture Content

Moisture content of lumber shall be not more than **19%** at the time of installation.

#### 9.3.2.6 — Lumber Dimensions

Lumber dimensions referred to in this Part are actual dimensions determined in conformance with **CSA O141** "Softwood Lumber."

#### 9.3.2.7 — Panel Thickness Tolerances

The thicknesses specified in this Part for plywood, hardboard, particleboard, OSB and waferboard shall be subject to the tolerances permitted in the standards referenced for these products unless specifically indicated herein.

#### 9.3.2.8 — Undersized Lumber

Joist, rafter, lintel and beam members up to **5% less** than the actual Canadian standard sizes are permitted to be used provided the allowable spans for the grade and species of lumber under consideration are reduced **5% from those shown in the Span Tables** for full size members.

#### 9.3.2.9 — Termite and Decay Protection

**Sentence 1** — In localities where termites are known to occur:
- Clearance between structural wood elements and the finished ground level directly below them shall be **not less than 450 mm**, and except as provided in Sentence (2), all sides of the supporting elements shall be visible to permit inspection, or
- Structural wood elements, supported by elements in contact with the ground or exposed over bare soil, shall be pressure-treated with a chemical that is toxic to termites.

**Sentence 2** — In localities where termites are known to occur AND foundations are insulated or otherwise finished in a manner that could conceal a termite infestation:
- A metal or plastic barrier shall be installed through the insulation and any other separation or finish materials above finished ground level to control the passage of termites behind or through the insulation, separation or finish materials, and
- Other separation or finish materials above finished ground level to control the passage of termites.

**Sentence 3** — Structural wood elements shall be **pressure-treated** with a preservative to resist decay:
- Where the vertical clearance between structural wood elements and the finished ground level is **less than 150 mm** (see also Articles 9.23.2.2 and 9.23.2.3), or
- Where:
  - The wood elements are not protected from exposure to precipitation,
  - The configuration is conducive to moisture accumulation, and
  - The moisture index is **greater than 1.00**

**Sentence 4** — Structural wood elements used in retaining walls and cribbing shall be pressure-treated to resist decay where:
- The retaining wall or cribbing supports ground that is critical to the stability of building foundations, or
- The retaining wall or cribbing is **greater than 1.2 m in height**.

**Sentence 5** — Where wood is required by this Article to be treated to resist termites or decay, such treatment shall be in accordance with Table 2, Use Categories for Specific Products, Uses, and Exposures, of **CAN/CSA-O80.1** "Specification of treated wood":
- **UC1** — interior construction, above-ground applications, wood member remains dry
- **UC2** — interior construction, above-ground applications, wood member may be subjected to occasional sources of moisture
- **UC3.2** — exterior construction, above-ground applications, wood member is uncoated or used in a configuration conducive to moisture accumulation
- **UC4.1** — wood member is in contact with the ground, in contact with fresh water, or vertical clearance to ground < 150 mm and wood not separated from permeable supporting materials by a moisture barrier
- **UC4.2** — wood member used in critical structural components, including permanent wood foundations

**Sentence 6** — Where wood is protected in accordance with UC1 or UC2 using an inorganic boron preservative, the wood shall be:
- Protected from direct exposure to water during and after the completion of construction, and
- Separated from permeable supporting materials by a moisture barrier that is resistant to all expected mechanisms of deterioration in the service environment if the vertical clearance to the ground is less than 150 mm.

**Sentence 7** — Wood required by this Article to be treated to resist termites or decay shall be identified by a mark to indicate the type of preservative used and conformance to the relevant required Use Category.

### 9.3.3 Metal

#### 9.3.3.1 — Sheet Metal Thickness

Minimum thicknesses for sheet metal material stated in this Part refer to the actual minimum **base metal thicknesses** measured at any point of the material and, in the case of galvanized steel, include the thickness of the galvanizing coating unless otherwise indicated.

#### 9.3.3.2 — Galvanized Sheet Steel

**Sentence 1** — Where sheet steel is required to be galvanized, it shall be metallic-coated with zinc or an alloy of 55% aluminum-zinc meeting the requirements of:
- **ASTM A653/A653M** "Standard Specification for Steel Sheet, Zinc-Coated (Galvanized) or Zinc-Iron Alloy-Coated (Galvannealed) by the Hot-Dip Process," or
- **ASTM A792/A792M** "Standard Specification for Steel Sheet, 55% Aluminum-Zinc Alloy-Coated by the Hot-Dip Process"

**Sentence 2** — Where galvanized sheet steel is intended for use in locations exposed to the weather or as a flashing material, it shall have a zinc coating not less than the **G90 [Z275]** coating designation or an aluminum-zinc alloy coating not less than the **AZM150** coating designation.

## Section 9.4 — Structural Requirements

### 9.4.1 — Structural Design Requirements and Application Limitations

#### 9.4.1.1 — General

**Sentence 1** — Subject to the application limitations defined elsewhere in this Part, structural members and their connections shall:
- Conform to requirements provided elsewhere in this Part
- Be designed according to good engineering practice such as that provided in **CWC 2014** "Engineering Guide for Wood Frame Construction," or
- Be designed according to Part 4 using the loads and deflection and vibration limits specified in Part 9 or Part 4

**Application conditions** (Subsection 9.4.2.1) — This Subsection applies to light-frame constructions whose wall, floor and roof planes are generally comprised of frames of small repetitive structural members, and where:
- The roof and wall planes are clad, sheathed or braced on at least one side
- The small repetitive structural members are spaced not more than **600 mm o.c.**
- The clear span of any structural member does not exceed **12.2 m**
- The maximum deflection of the structural roof members conforms to Article 9.4.3.1
- The maximum total roof area, notwithstanding any separation of adjoining buildings by firewalls, is **4 550 m²**
- For flat roofs, there are no significant obstructions on the roof (such as parapet walls) spaced closer than the distance calculated by:

  **D₀ = 10(H₀ − 0.8S₀/γ)**

  Where:
  - D₀ = minimum distance between obstructions, m
  - H₀ = height of the obstruction above the roof, m
  - S₀ = ground snow load, kPa
  - γ = specific weight of snow taken as 4.0 kN/m³ or 0.43S₀ + 2.2 kN/m³, whichever is lesser

**Live load limit** — Where floor framing is designed in accordance with Clause (1)(b) or (c), and where supporting wall framing and fastenings, or footings are designed according to Clause (1)(a), the maximum specified live load on the floor according to Table 4.1.5.3 shall not exceed **2.4 kPa**.

**Location-specific data** — Location-specific information for structural design, including snow and wind loads and seismic design parameter, shall be determined according to Subsection 1.1.3.

---

### 9.4.2 — Specified Loads

#### 9.4.2.2 — Specified Snow Loads

**Sentence 1** — Except as provided in Sentences (2) to (4), specified snow loads shall be not less than those calculated using:

**S = C_b × S_s + S_r**

Where:
- S = specified snow load
- C_b = basic snow load roof factor: **0.45** where the entire roof width does not exceed 4.3 m; **0.55** for all other roofs
- S_s = 1-in-50-year ground snow load in kPa (per Subsection 1.1.3)
- S_r = associated 1-in-50-year rain load in kPa (per Subsection 1.1.3)

**Sentence 2** — In no case shall the specified snow load be less than **1 kPa**.

**Sentence 3** — Bow string, arch or semi-circular roof trusses having an unsupported span greater than **6 m** shall be designed in conformance with the snow load requirements in Subsection 4.1.6.

**Sentence 4** — Where the height of a roof step at the intersection of an upper level roof and lower level roof is greater than **2 m**, and the upper level roof has a slope less than 1 in 6 and an area greater than **600 m²**, the specified snow load on the lower level roof shall be:
- For distances ≤ drift length x_d: not less than **1.5× the specified snow load S** (with C_b = 0.55)
- For distances > drift length x_d: as specified in Sentence (1)

**Sentence 5** — The drift length x_d (m):

**x_d = 5(h − 0.55S_s/γ)**

Where: h = height of the roof step (m); γ = specific weight of snow per Clause 9.4.2.1(1)(f)

#### 9.4.2.3 — Platforms Subject to Snow and Occupancy Loads

Balconies, decks and other accessible exterior platforms intended for an occupancy and subject to snow loads shall be designed to carry the specified roof snow load or **1.9 kPa**, whichever is greater, where the platform, or each segregated area of the platform, serves a single dwelling unit.

#### 9.4.2.4 — Attics and Roof Spaces

Ceiling joists or truss bottom chords in residential attic or roof spaces having limited accessibility that precludes the storage of equipment or material shall be designed for a total specified load of not less than **0.35 kPa** (dead load + live load of the ceiling).

#### 9.4.2.5 — Seismic Design Parameter

**Sentence 1** — Unless otherwise indicated, the value of the seismic design parameter, S_max, at a location listed in Table C-3 of Appendix C shall be taken as S_max for the unknown Site Class.

**Sentence 2** — Where the Site Class is determined in accordance with Sentence 4.1.8.4(3), the value of the seismic design parameter, S_(max,sc), at a location listed in Table C-3 of Appendix C, is permitted to be taken as S_max for the value for the determined Site Class.

### 9.4.4 — Foundation Conditions

#### 9.4.4.1 — Allowable Bearing Pressures

Footing sizes for shallow foundations shall be determined in accordance with Section 9.15, or designed in accordance with Section 4.2 using the maximum allowable bearing pressures in Table 9.4.4.1 or allowable bearing pressures from subsurface investigation.

**Table 9.4.4.1 — Allowable Bearing Pressure for Soil or Rock:**

| Type and Condition of Soil or Rock | Maximum Allowable Bearing Pressure (kPa) |
|---|---|
| Dense or compact sand or gravel | 150 |
| Loose sand or gravel | 50 |
| Dense or compact silt | 100 |
| Stiff clay | 150 |
| Firm clay | 75 |
| Soft clay | 40 |
| Till | 200 |
| Clay shale | 300 |
| Sound rock | 500 |

*Note (1): See Note A-Table 9.4.4.1.*

#### 9.4.4.2 — Foundation Capacity in Weaker Soil and Rock

Where soil or rock within a distance equal to **twice the footing width** below the bearing surface has a lower allowable bearing pressure than that at the bearing surface, the design capacity of the foundation shall not be greater than would cause the weakest soil or rock to be stressed beyond its allowable bearing pressure. Subsurface loads are assumed distributed uniformly over a horizontal plane within a frustum extending downward from the footing at an angle of **60°** to the horizontal.

#### 9.4.4.3 — High Water Table

Where a foundation bears on gravel, sand or silt, and the water table is within a distance below the bearing surface equal to the width of the foundation, the allowable bearing pressure shall be **50%** of that determined in Article 9.4.4.1.

#### 9.4.4.4 — Soil Movement

Where a foundation is located in an area where soil movement caused by changes in soil moisture content, freezing, or chemical-microbiological oxidation is known to occur to the extent that it will damage a building, measures shall be taken to preclude such movement or to reduce its effects. The potential for slope instability shall be evaluated based on site-specific material properties and ground motion parameters referenced in Subsection 1.1.3.

#### 9.4.4.5 — Retaining Walls

Walls shall be designed to resist the lateral pressure of the retained material.

#### 9.4.4.6 — Walls Supporting Drained Earth

**Sentence 1** — Except where constructed in accordance with Section 9.15, walls supporting **drained earth** shall be designed:
- For a pressure equivalent to that exerted by a fluid with density ≥ **480 kg/m³** and depth equal to that of the retained earth, or
- In accordance with Section 4.2 to resist the loads and effects described in Article 4.1.2.1.

**Sentence 2** — Walls supporting **other than drained earth** shall be designed:
- For the pressure in Clause (1)(a) plus the fluid pressure of the surcharge, or
- In accordance with Section 4.2 to resist the loads and effects described in Article 4.1.2.1.

## Section 9.5 — Design of Areas and Spaces

### 9.5.1 — General

#### 9.5.1.1 — Method of Measurement

Dimensions of rooms or spaces shall be measured between **finished wall surfaces** and between **finished floor and ceiling surfaces**.

#### 9.5.1.2 — Combination Rooms

Two or more areas may be considered a combination room if the opening between the areas occupies the larger of **3 m²** or **40%** of the area of the wall measured on the side of the dependent area. Where the dependent area is a bedroom, direct passage shall be provided between the two areas.

---

### 9.5.2 — Accessible Design

#### 9.5.2.1 — General

Except as provided in Articles 9.5.2.3 and 3.8.2.1, every building shall be designed in conformance with **Section 3.8**.

#### 9.5.2.2 — Protection on Floor Areas with an Accessible Path of Travel

Where an accessible path of travel required in Article 9.5.2.1 is provided to any storey above the first storey, the requirements in Article 3.3.1.7 shall apply.

#### 9.5.2.3 — Apartment Buildings

**Sentence 1** — In a building of residential occupancy with multiple dwelling units and common interior space served by a common building entrance, access described in Section 3.8 shall be provided from the entrances throughout all common spaces of entrance storeys and within all other common spaces including rooftop occupancies serving adaptable dwelling units.

**Sentence 2** — Access required by Sentence (1) need not be provided on a floor level that:
- Is not served by a ramp, passenger elevator, platform-equipped passenger-elevating device, escalator or inclined moving walk,
- Is not a building entrance level, and
- Does not contain common facilities that are not also provided on an accessible level.

#### 9.5.2.4 — Visible Signal Devices

Visible signal devices shall be installed in conformance with Articles **3.2.4.19** and **3.2.4.20**.

#### 9.5.2.5 — Lighting

Notwithstanding the requirements of Subsection 9.34.2, illumination shall be provided in accordance with **Article 3.2.7.1**.

#### 9.5.3.2 — Mezzanines

The ceiling height above and below a mezzanine floor assembly in occupancies **other than residential occupancies** shall be not less than **2.1 m**.

#### 9.5.3.3 — Storage Garages

The clear height in a storage garage shall be not less than **2 m**.

### 9.5.4 — Hallways

#### 9.5.4.1 — Hallway Width

The unobstructed width of a hallway within a dwelling unit shall be not less than **860 mm**, except permitted to be **710 mm** where:
- There are only bedrooms and bathrooms at the end of the hallway furthest from the living area, AND
- A second exit is provided:
  - In the hallway near the end farthest from the living area, or
  - In each bedroom served by the hallway.

---

### 9.5.5 — Doorway Sizes

#### 9.5.5.1 — Doorway Opening Sizes

## Section 9.7 — Windows, Doors and Skylights

### 9.7.1 — General

#### 9.7.1.1 — Application

**Sentence 1** — This Section applies to:
- Windows, doors and skylights separating conditioned space from unconditioned space or the exterior, and
- Entrance doors to dwelling units.

**Sentence 2** — "Skylight" = unit skylights, roof windows and tubular daylighting devices.

**Sentence 3** — "Doors" includes glazing in doors and sidelights for doors but does NOT include vehicular access doors.

---

### 9.7.2 — Required Windows, Doors and Skylights

#### 9.7.2.1 — Entrance Doors

A door shall be provided at each entrance to a dwelling unit. Main entrance doors to dwelling units shall be provided with:
- A door viewer or transparent glazing in the door, or
- A sidelight.

---

### 9.7.3 — Performance of Windows, Doors and Skylights

#### 9.7.3.1 — General

Skylights and their components shall be designed, constructed and installed so that they resist snow loads.

#### 9.7.3.2 — Heat Transfer Performance

Windows, doors and skylights and their components described in Sentence 9.7.1.1(1) shall be designed, constructed and installed to:
- Minimize surface condensation on the warm side of the component, and
- Ensure comfortable conditions for occupants.

Compliance demonstrated by:
- Complying with the requirements in Article 9.7.3.3, or
- Design and construction conforming to Part 5.

#### 9.7.3.3 — Thermal Characteristics of Windows, Doors and Skylights

**Sentence 1** — Except as permitted in Sentence (2), metal frames and sash of windows, doors and skylights shall incorporate a **thermal break**.

**Sentence 2** — Windows and doors described in Sentence (1) do not require a thermal break where they are installed as:
- Reserved
- Storm windows and doors, or
- Are required to have a fire-protection rating.

**Sentence 3** — Windows, doors and skylights with or without storm doors or sash installed in portions of buildings where the intended use of the interior space will not result in high moisture generation shall be designed in conformance with Section 5.3.

**Sentence 4** — Reserved.

---

### 9.7.4 — Design and Construction

#### 9.7.4.1 — General

Except as provided by Sentence (2), windows, doors, skylights and their components shall be designed and constructed in accordance with:
- Article 9.7.4.2, or
- Part 5.

Windows, doors, skylights and their components that are required to have a **fire-protection rating** need not conform to this Subsection.

#### 9.7.4.2 — Standards

**Sentence 1** — Except as permitted by Sentence (2) and Article 9.7.4.3, windows, doors and skylights and their components shall conform to:
- **AAMA/WDMA/CSA 101/I.S.2/A440** "North American Fenestration Standard/Specification for windows, doors, and skylights" (Harmonized Standard), and
- **CSA A44051** "Canadian Supplement to AAMA/WDMA/CSA 101/I.S.2/A440, North American Fenestration Standard/Specification for windows, doors, and skylights"

**Sentence 2** — A door designated as a "Limited Water" door in accordance with the standard referenced in Clause (1)(a) shall not be used unless the door:
- Separates a dwelling unit from an unconditioned storage garage or a carport
- Is designed with a clear width, a clear and level space, a door-opening device and a door closer in conformance with Subsection 3.8.3.6, or
- Meets the criteria in Sentence 9.27.3.8(3) such that flashing would not be required.

#### 9.7.4.3 — Performance Requirements

**Sentence 1** — For buildings of no more than **10 m in height** (measured from grade), windows, doors and their components may conform to the design pressure, performance grade and water resistance values in **Table C-5 of Appendix C** instead of the values calculated in CSA A44051.

**Sentence 2** — For buildings described in Sentence 1.3.3.3(1) of Division A, the driving rain wind pressure (DRWP) values in **Table A.1 of CSA A44051** shall be used.

---

### 9.7.5 — Resistance to Forced Entry

#### 9.7.5.1 — Resistance to Forced Entry for Sliding Doors

Applies to sliding doors serving dwelling units, other than exterior doors to garages and other ancillary spaces.

- **Sentence 2** — Sliding doors shall not permit the removal of the sliding panel when in the locked position.
- **Sentence 3** — Exterior doors shall:
  - Have a pin-type locking mechanism with a minimum **9 mm throw** into the frame, operable from the interior without keys, special devices or specialized knowledge, or
  - Conform to at least **Grade 10** in **ASTM F842** "Standard Test Methods for Measuring the Forced Entry Resistance of Sliding Door Assemblies, Excluding Glazing Impact."

#### 9.7.5.2 — Resistance to Forced Entry for Swinging Doors

**Sentence 1** — Except for exterior doors to garages and to other ancillary spaces, this Article applies to:
- Swinging entrance doors to dwelling units
- Swinging doors between dwelling units and attached garages or other ancillary spaces
- Swinging doors that provide access directly or indirectly from a storage garage to a dwelling unit

**Sentence 2** — Doors, frames and hardware conforming to a security level of at least **Grade 10** as described in the Annex to **ASTM F476** "Standard Test Methods for Security of Swinging Door Assemblies" are NOT required to conform to Sentences (3) to (7).

**Sentence 3** — Except as permitted in Sentence (2), wood doors as described in Sentence (1) shall:
- Be solid core or stile-and-rail type
- Be not less than **45 mm thick**, and
- If of the stile-and-rail type, have a panel thickness of not less than **19 mm**, with a total panel area not more than **half** of the door area.

**Sentence 4** — Except as permitted in Sentence (2), doors described in Sentence (1) shall be provided with:
- A **deadbolt** with a cylinder having no fewer than **5 pins**, and
- A bolt throw not less than **25 mm long**, protected with a solid or hardened free-turning ring or beveled cylinder housing.

**Sentence 5** — Except as permitted in Sentence (2), an inactive leaf in double doors used in locations specified in Sentence (1) shall be provided with heavy-duty bolts top and bottom having an engagement of not less than **15 mm**.

**Sentence 6** — Except as permitted in Sentence (2), hinges for doors described in Sentence (1) shall be fastened:
- To wood doors with wood screws not less than **25 mm long**, and to wood frames with wood screws so that at least **2 screws per hinge** penetrate not less than **30 mm into solid wood**, or
- To metal doors and metal frames with machine screws not smaller than **No. 10** and not less than **10 mm long**.

**Sentence 7** — Strikeplates for deadbolts described in Sentence (4) shall be fastened:
- To wood frames with wood screws penetrating not less than **30 mm into solid wood**, or
- To metal frames with machine screws not smaller than **No. 8** and not less than **10 mm long**.

**Sentence 8** — **Solid blocking** shall be provided on both sides at the lock height between the jambs for doors described in Sentence (1) and the structural framing so that the jambs will resist spreading by force.

**Sentence 9** — Except for screen or storm doors, doors described in Sentence (1) that **swing outward** shall be provided with hinges or pins so that the doors cannot be removed when they are in the closed position.

#### 9.7.5.3 — Resistance to Forced Entry for Windows

In dwelling units, windows, any part of which is located within **2 m of adjacent ground level**, shall conform to the requirements for resistance to forced entry as described in **Clause 5.3.6 of AAMA/WDMA/CSA 101/I.S.2/A440**.

### 9.7.6 — Installation

#### 9.7.6.1 — Installation of Windows, Doors and Skylights

- Installation of manufactured/pre-assembled windows, doors and skylights shall conform to the manufacturer's instructions, if any.
- In case of conflict between the Code and manufacturer's instructions, **the Code governs**.
- Windows, doors and skylights shall be **sealed to air barriers**.

#### 9.7.6.2 — Sealants, Trim and Flashing

- **Sentence 1** — Sealing compound used to seal the glass component of an insulating glazing unit to the sash shall be compatible with the sealing compound used to edge-seal the glass component.
- **Sentence 2** — Flashing used to protect openings shall conform to **Articles 9.27.3.7 and 9.27.3.8**.
- **Sentence 3** — Sealants shall be applied between window frames or trim and the exterior cladding or masonry in conformance with **Subsection 9.27.4**.
- **Sentence 4** — All unfinished portions of the frame and other components of aluminum windows, doors or skylights in contact with the edges of masonry, concrete, stucco or plaster shall be protected with an **alkali-resistant coating**.

## Section 9.8 — Stairs, Ramps, Landings, Handrails and Guards

### 9.8.1 — Application

#### 9.8.1.1 — General

This Section applies to the design and construction of interior and exterior stairs, steps, ramps, landings, handrails and guards.

#### 9.8.1.2 — Stairs, Ramps, Landings, Handrails and Guards in Garages

Where stairs, ramps, landings, handrails or guards are installed in garages that serve a single dwelling unit or a house with a secondary suite including their common spaces, the garage shall be considered to be part of the dwelling unit and the requirements for stairs, ramps, landings, handrails and guards within dwelling units shall apply.

#### 9.8.1.3 — Exit Stairs, Ramps and Landings

Where a stair, ramp or landing forms part of an exit, the appropriate requirements in **Sections 9.9 and 9.10** shall also apply.

#### 9.8.1.4 — Escalators and Moving Walkways

Escalators and moving walkways shall conform to the appropriate requirements in **Part 3**.

#### 9.8.1.5 — Tactile Walking Surface Indicators

Tactile attention indicators shall be installed in conformance with **Article 3.3.1.19**.

---

### 9.8.2 — Stair Dimensions

#### 9.8.2.1 — Stair Width

- **Buildings of residential occupancy** — required exit stairs and public stairs: ≥ **900 mm** wide.
- **Single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite** (including common spaces) — exit stairs: ≥ **860 mm** wide.
- **Buildings other than residential occupancy** — required exit stairs and public stairs: ≥ greater of **900 mm** or **8 mm per person** per Table 3.1.17.1.
- **At least one stair** between each floor level within a dwelling unit, and exterior stairs serving a single dwelling unit (except required exit stairs): ≥ **860 mm** wide.

#### 9.8.2.2 — Height over Stairs

- Clear height over stairs measured vertically, over the clear width of the stair, from a straight line tangent to tread and landing nosings to the lowest point above.
- General minimum: ≥ **2 050 mm**.
- Over stairs serving a **single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite** (including common spaces): ≥ **1 950 mm**.

---

### 9.8.3 — Stair Configurations

#### 9.8.3.1 — Permitted Configurations

- **Buildings other than dwelling units** and houses with secondary suite: straight flights, or curved flights per Sentence (4).
- **Within dwelling units and houses with secondary suite**: straight flights, curved flights per Sentence (4), flights with rectangular treads and winders (except only one set of winders per floor level change), or reserved configurations.
- All **tapered treads** within a flight shall turn in the **same direction**.
- **Curved flights in exits**: shall comply with Sentence 3.4.6.9(2).

#### 9.8.3.2 — Minimum Number of Risers

Except for stairs within a dwelling unit, at least **3 risers** shall be provided in interior flights.

#### 9.8.3.3 — Maximum Height of Stairs

The vertical height of any flight of stairs shall not exceed **3.7 m**.

---

### 9.8.4 — Step Dimensions

#### 9.8.4.1 — Dimensions for Risers (Table 9.8.4.1)

| Stair Type | Max Rise (mm) | Min Rise (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| Private *(1)* | 200 | 125 |
| Public *(2)* | 180 | 125 |

*(1) Private = serves single dwelling units; houses with secondary suite including common spaces; garages associated with those houses.*
*(2) Public = all stairs not described as service stairs or private stairs.*

For stairs serving service rooms or service spaces: rise measured as the vertical nosing-to-nosing distance.

#### 9.8.4.2 — Dimensions for Rectangular Treads (Table 9.8.4.2)

| Stair Type | Max Run (mm) | Min Run (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| Private *(1)* | 355 | 255 |
| Public *(2)* | No limit | 280 |

Depth of a rectangular tread shall be not less than its run and not more than its run plus **25 mm**.

#### 9.8.4.3 — Dimensions of Tapered Treads

Tapered treads shall have a run that:
- Is not less than **150 mm** at the narrow end of the tread, and
- Complies with Table 9.8.4.2 when measured at a point **300 mm from the centre line of the handrail** at the narrow end of the tread.

Tapered treads in required exit stairs: per Article 3.4.6.9.

Depth of a tapered tread shall be not less than its run at any point and not more than its run plus **25 mm** at any point.

#### 9.8.4.4 — Uniformity and Tolerances for Risers, Runs and Treads

**Risers** (uniform height in any flight):
- Max tolerance: **5 mm** between adjacent treads or landings
- Max tolerance: **10 mm** between tallest and shortest risers in a flight
- Exception: for required exit stairs where top or bottom riser adjoins a sloping walking surface (garage floor, driveway, sidewalk): height of riser across that step shall vary by not more than **1 in 12**.

**Rectangular treads** (uniform run):
- Max tolerance: **5 mm** between adjacent treads
- Max tolerance: **10 mm** between deepest and shallowest treads in a flight

**Tapered treads**: uniform run in accordance with tolerances in Sentence (3), measured at a point **300 mm from the centre line of the handrail**.

**Slope of treads**: shall not exceed **1 in 50**.

#### 9.8.4.6 — Winders

- Individual treads in winders shall turn through an angle of **30°** (no deviation above or below 30°) or **45°** (no deviation above or below 45°).
- Where winders are incorporated into a stair, each set shall not turn through more than **90°**.
- Treads in winders: run measured at **200 mm from the narrow end** of the tread, conforming to minimum run for a private stair in Table 9.8.4.2.

#### 9.8.4.8 — Tread Nosings

- Top of nosings of stair treads shall have a **rounded or beveled edge** extending not less than **6 mm** and not more than **14 mm** horizontally from the front of the nosing.
- If resilient material covers the nosing: minimum extension reduced to **3 mm**.

#### 9.8.4.9 — Open Risers

Stairs shall have **no open risers** except for:
- Interior and exterior stairs serving a single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite
- Fire escape stairs
- Stairs principally used for maintenance
- Stairs serving service rooms
- Stairs serving industrial occupancies other than storage garages

---

### 9.8.5 — Ramps

**Application**: Applies to pedestrian ramps, except ramps in an accessible path of travel (those must comply with Article 3.8.3.5).

#### 9.8.5.2 — Ramp Width
- General: ≥ **1 100 mm** wide.
- Single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite (including common spaces): ≥ **860 mm** wide.

#### 9.8.5.3 — Height over Ramps
- General: ≥ **2 050 mm**.
- Single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite: ≥ **1 950 mm**.

#### 9.8.5.4 — Ramp Slope (maximum)

| Context | Max Slope |
|---|---|
| Exterior ramps | 1 in 10 |
| Interior ramps serving residential occupancies | 1 in 10 |
| Industrial occupancies | 1 in 6 |
| All other occupancies | 1 in 8 |

---

### 9.8.6 — Landings

**Application**: Applies to landings except for ramps in an accessible path of travel (those per Article 3.8.3.5). Ground surfaces with slope ≤ **1 in 50** at top and bottom of stairs or ramps are considered landings.

#### 9.8.6.2 — Required Landings

A landing shall be provided:
- At top and bottom of each flight of interior and exterior stairs (including garage stairs)
- At top and bottom of every ramp with slope > 1 in 50
- Where a doorway opens onto a stair or ramp
- Where a ramp opens onto a stair
- Where a stair opens onto a ramp

**Exceptions:**
- Door at top of stair within a dwelling unit **swings away** from the stair → no landing required between doorway and stair.
- Omit at top of exterior flight serving a **secondary entrance** to a single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite IF: stair ≤ **3 risers**, principal door is sliding or swings away from stair, and storm/screen door (if any) swings over stair with hardware to hold it open.
- Omit at **bottom** of exterior stair or ramp IF no obstruction within lesser of stair/ramp width, or:
  - **900 mm** for stairs/ramps serving single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite
  - **1 100 mm** for all other stairs/ramps

#### 9.8.6.3 — Dimensions of Landings

- Landings shall be at least as wide and as long as the width of the stair or ramp in which they occur.
- Where landing does not turn or turns < 90°: length need not exceed lesser of required width of stair/ramp or **1 100 mm**.
- Length measured perpendicular to nosings of adjacent steps or end of ramp, at distance equal to half the length required from the narrow edge.
- Where stair flights or ramps of different widths adjoin one landing:
  - If one or more widths ≤ required widths: landing ≥ the greater required stair/ramp width
  - If all widths > required widths: landing ≥ the lesser actual stair/ramp width
- Where door swings toward a stair: full arc of swing shall be over the landing.
- Slope of landings: shall not exceed **1 in 50**.
- Where doorway or stairway opens onto the **side of a ramp**: landing shall extend ≥ **300 mm** on either side (except on a side abutting an end wall).

#### 9.8.6.4 — Height over Landings
- General: ≥ **2 050 mm**.
- Single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite: ≥ **1 950 mm**.

---

### 9.8.7 — Handrails

#### 9.8.7.1 — Required Handrails

Handrails shall be installed on stairs and ramps in accordance with Table 9.8.7.1.

**Table 9.8.7.1 — Number of Sides of Stair or Ramp Required to Have a Handrail:**

| Location | Stairs < 1 100 mm Wide (Straight) | Stairs < 1 100 mm Wide (Curved) | Stairs ≥ 1 100 mm Wide (All) | Ramps < 1 100 mm Wide (Str. or Curved) | Ramps ≥ 1 100 mm Wide (All) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Within a dwelling unit or house with secondary suite | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| All other locations | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

**Additional rules:**
- Except where a stair or ramp serves not more than **two dwelling units**, at least one handrail shall be located not more than **750 mm** from the natural path of travel on the stair or ramp.
- Handrails **not required** for stairs/ramps serving a single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite (including common spaces) where:
  - Interior stairs have **not more than 2 risers**
  - Exterior stairs have **not more than 3 risers**, or
  - Ramps rise not more than **400 mm**
- Only **one handrail** required on exterior stairs with > 3 risers serving ≤ one dwelling unit or house with secondary suite.
- For flights of tapered treads or mixed tapered/rectangular treads within a dwelling unit or house with secondary suite: **one handrail shall be installed along the narrow end of the treads**.

#### 9.8.7.2 — Continuity of Handrails

- Required handrails shall be continuously graspable throughout:
  - Ramps (full length)
  - Flights of stairs, from the **bottom riser to the top riser**
- Except for stairs/ramps serving a single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite: at least one required handrail shall be continuous throughout the length of the stair/ramp **including at the landing**, except where interrupted by doorways.
- For stairs/ramps serving a single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite: a handrail is permitted to start from a **newel post or volute** installed on the bottom tread.

#### 9.8.7.3 — Termination of Handrails

- Handrails shall terminate in a manner that will not obstruct pedestrian travel or create a hazard.
- Except for stairs and ramps serving only one dwelling unit or house with secondary suite: at least one required handrail shall extend horizontally not less than **300 mm beyond the top and bottom** of each flight or ramp.

#### 9.8.7.4 — Height of Handrails (Measurement)

The height of handrails on stairs and ramps shall be measured vertically from the top of the handrail to:
- A straight line drawn **tangent to the tread nosings** of the stair served by the handrail
- The **surface of the ramp, floor or landing** served by the handrail

#### 9.8.7.5 — Height Range and Ergonomic Design

**Height range:**
- Required handrails: **865 mm to 1 070 mm** high (exceptions: Sentence (3) of 9.8.7.5 and Clause 3.8.3.5(1)(e)).
- Handrails installed in addition to required handrails need not comply with the height range.

**Ergonomic design:**
- Clearance between a handrail and the surface behind it: not less than **50 mm** (or **60 mm** if the surface is rough or abrasive).
- All handrails shall be continuously graspable along their entire length with **no obstruction on or above them** to break a handhold.

#### 9.8.7.6 — Projections into Stairs and Ramps

Handrails and constructions below handrails (including handrail supports and stair stringers) shall not project more than **100 mm** into the required width of a stair or ramp.

#### 9.8.7.7 — Design and Attachment of Handrails

Handrails and their supports shall withstand the following loads (not required to be considered simultaneously):
- Concentrated load ≥ **0.9 kN** applied at any point in any direction (all handrails)
- Uniform load ≥ **0.7 kN/m** (for handrails other than those serving a single dwelling unit)

For handrails serving a single dwelling unit or house with secondary suite attached to **wood studs or blocking**, the attachment is deemed compliant with the above if:
- Attachment points spaced ≤ **1.2 m** apart (horizontal plane)
- First attachment point at either end ≤ **300 mm** from end of handrail
- Fasteners: ≥ **2 No. 8 wood screws** at each point, penetrating ≥ **32 mm** into solid wood

### 9.8.8 — Guards

#### 9.8.8.1 — Required Guards

**Sentence 1** — Except as provided in Sentence (2) and except at the leading edge at the top of a flight of steps and ramps, every surface to which access is provided (including but not limited to flights of steps and ramps, exterior landings, porches, balconies, mezzanines, galleries and raised walkways) shall be protected by a guard on each side that is not protected by a wall **for the length where the difference in elevation is more than 600 mm** between the walking surface and the adjacent surface within **1.2 m**.

**Sentence 2** — Guards are **not required**:
- At loading docks
- At floor pits in repair garages
- Where access is provided for maintenance purposes only

**Sentence 3** — Doors in buildings of residential occupancy, where the finished floor on one side of the door is more than **600 mm** above the floor or other constructed surface or ground level on the other side, shall be protected by:
- A guard, or
- A mechanism capable of controlling the free swinging or sliding of the door so as to limit any clear unobstructed opening to not more than **100 mm**

**Sentence 4** — Except as provided in Sentence (5), openable windows in buildings of residential occupancy shall be protected by:
- A guard, or
- A mechanism that can only be released with the use of tools or special knowledge to control the free swinging or sliding operation of the openable part of the window so as to limit any clear unobstructed opening to not more than **100 mm** measured either vertically or horizontally

**Sentence 5** — Windows need **not** be protected in accordance with Sentence (4) where the bottom edge of the openable portion of the window is located:
- More than **900 mm** above the finished floor, or
- Less than **1 800 mm** above the floor or ground on the other side of the window

**Sentence 6** — Except as provided in Sentence (7), glazing installed over stairs, ramps and landings that extends to less than **1 070 mm** above the surface of the treads, ramp or landing shall be:
- Protected by guards in accordance with this Subsection, or
- Non-openable and designed to withstand the specified lateral loads for **balcony guards** as provided in Article 4.1.5.14

**Sentence 7** — In dwelling units and houses with secondary suite (including common spaces), glazing installed over stairs, ramps and landings that extends to less than **900 mm** above the surface of the treads, ramp or landing shall be:
- Protected by guards, or
- Non-openable and designed to withstand the specified lateral loads for **balcony guards** per Article 4.1.5.14

**Sentence 8** — Glazing installed in public areas that extends to less than **1 m** from the floor and is located above the second storey in buildings of residential occupancy shall be:
- Protected by guards, or
- Non-openable and designed to withstand the specified lateral loads for **balcony guards** per Article 4.1.5.14

## Notes for Code Application

1. **"Private" vs "Public" stairs**: Private stairs serve single dwelling units, houses with secondary suite (including common spaces), and associated garages. Public stairs are all stairs not described as service stairs or private stairs.

2. **"Secondary suite"**: A secondary suite is an additional dwelling unit within a house; it significantly affects which (dwelling-unit-level vs. public) requirements apply throughout Section 9.8.

3. **Accessible path of travel**: Part 3 requirements (Section 3.8, Article 3.8.3.5) take precedence over Part 9 for accessible ramps and landings.

4. **Code governs over manufacturer instructions**: Where manufacturer's installation instructions conflict with this Code, the Code governs (Article 9.7.6.1(2)).

5. **Appendix A Notes**: Many articles reference Appendix A Notes (e.g., Note A-9.7.5.2(1)) which provide explanatory guidance. These are not reproduced here but should be consulted for ambiguous or complex situations.

6. **Location-specific data**: Snow loads (S_s, S_r), seismic parameters (S_max), and soil conditions require location-specific data from Appendix C, Table C-3, and Subsection 1.1.3. These values cannot be provided without a specific BC municipality or coordinates.

7. **Exit stair provisions**: Where a stair/ramp/landing is part of a required exit, Part 9 requirements are supplemented by Sections 9.9 and 9.10 and relevant Part 3 provisions.

8. **Flat ICF walls**: The concrete and reinforcing specifications in Article 9.3.1.1 are specific to flat ICF walls ≤ 2 storeys with max 3 m floor-to-floor in light-frame buildings; other concrete wall types must comply with Part 4.

9. **Panel thickness tolerances** (Article 9.3.2.7): Plywood, hardboard, particleboard, OSB and waferboard thicknesses are subject to tolerances in the referenced product standards unless specifically indicated otherwise in this Part.
